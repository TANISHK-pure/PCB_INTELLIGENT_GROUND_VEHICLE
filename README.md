## IGV Custom Motherboard
A custom-designed 2-layer printed circuit board (PCB) built to serve as the central nervous system for an Intelligent Ground Vehicle (IGV) competing in the Gujcost Robofest 5.0 for final Stage. I built this hardware project to bridge the gap between high-performance 3.3V microcontrollers and rugged 5V robotics hardware safely and reliably.

📦 Technologies

Teensy 4.0

🦄 Features
Here's what this custom motherboard does for the IGV:

Centralized Power Distribution: Dedicated thick traces (1.0mm+) to safely route main battery input (VBAT) to cooling fans and external 5V regulated input to sensors without overheating.

Logic Level Shifting: Integrated an ET5244 4-channel bi-directional converter to safely translate 3.3V signals from the Teensy brain to the 5V logic required by the optical encoders.

Plug-and-Play Motor Control: Custom male/female header breakouts for front and rear motor drivers, allowing fast hot-swapping of PWM and DIR control lines during the competition.

EMI Noise Reduction: Full top and bottom layer ground pours (copper flooding) to absorb electrical noise generated by the heavy DC motors and protect sensitive logic signals.

Safe Mounting: Strategically placed 3.2mm multi-layer mounting holes in all four corners with proper clearance from active copper tracks to prevent short circuits on the metal chassis.

👨‍🍳 The Process
I started by designing the full schematic in EasyEDA, mapping out the logic connections between the Teensy 4.0, the logic shifters, and the external headers for the motor drivers and encoders.

Next, I converted the schematic into a PCB layout. The biggest challenge was spatial organization. I grouped the board into distinct zones: a Power Zone, a Brain Zone (Teensy), a Translator Zone (Logic Shifter), and a Sensor/Muscle Zone.

When routing the copper tracks, I had to strictly manage trace widths based on current draw. I used 0.3mm traces for high-speed PWM and encoder data, and thick 0.6mm to 1.0mm traces for 3.3V/5V power delivery.

To connect all the ground pins without creating a messy web of wires, I utilized a top and bottom layer Copper Pour, effectively creating a solid Ground Plane. This required using vias and bottom-layer routing to connect "copper islands."

Finally, I ran a strict Design Rule Check (DRC). After achieving 0 DRC errors, I generated the final Gerber files and verified the physical dimensions (96.77 mm × 70.23 mm) to ensure it would mount perfectly on the robot.

📚 What I Learned
During this project, I picked up critical hardware engineering skills and a deep understanding of physical electronic constraints.

🧠 Trace Width Management:

Current Handling: I learned that not all electricity flows the same. I had to calculate and apply different trace widths (0.3mm for data, 1.0mm for power) to prevent traces from overheating like fuses.

⚡ Signal Integrity & Translation:

Voltage Bridging: I learned how to properly interface 3.3V microcontrollers with 5V industrial sensors using logic level converters without frying the main brain.

🌊 Ground Planes & Copper Pouring:

Noise Mitigation: I learned how to flood empty PCB space with ground copper. Tracking down isolated "copper islands" and fixing them with bottom-layer tunnels taught me how to think in 3D about electrical routing.

💭 How can it be improved?
Add an onboard reverse-polarity protection diode to prevent damage if the battery is plugged in backward.

Integrate the 5V Buck Converter directly onto the PCB instead of using an external module.

Add LED status indicators for each of the power rails (VBAT, 5V, 3V3) for faster hardware debugging.

Upgrade to a 4-layer board in the future to completely isolate signal tracks from power planes.

🚦 Manufacturing the Project
To fabricate this board, the exported Gerber .zip file is designed for JLCPCB with the following specifications:

Base Material: FR-4

Layers: 2

Dimensions: 96.77 mm * 70.23 mm

PCB Thickness: 1.6mm (for high rigidity)

Copper Weight: 1 oz

Surface Finish: HASL (with lead) for easier hand-soldering.
